InstaBlock vs Screen Time vs app blockers (Opal, one sec, ClearSpace)

The difference is what gets blocked. Screen Time and app blockers such as Opal, one sec and ClearSpace work on the whole app — Instagram is either available or it isn't. InstaBlock works on the traffic: it stops Instagram Reels and TikTok videos from loading while messages, stories and photos keep working, and it has no off switch to bypass.

When an app blocker is the better tool

If your goal is "no Instagram at all during work hours" or "a breathing pause before every social app", Screen Time or an app blocker does that well, and one sec's pause-before-opening approach is genuinely effective for impulse opens. They also cover apps InstaBlock doesn't touch.

When InstaBlock is

If the problem is specifically Reels and TikTok — the infinite video feed — while you still need Instagram for messages, group chats, stories or your own posts, an app-level block forces a bad trade. InstaBlock removes the feed and keeps the rest. It's also the only option here with no bypass beyond a timed break.

Using both

They don't conflict. Some people keep a Screen Time schedule for overnight and run InstaBlock all day so Instagram stays usable but un-scrollable.

Questions

What's the difference between InstaBlock and Screen Time?

Screen Time blocks or limits whole apps. InstaBlock blocks only Instagram Reels and TikTok video traffic, so DMs, stories and photos keep working, and it has no 'Ignore Limit' bypass.

Is InstaBlock like Opal or one sec?

No. Those are app blockers that shield or pause whole apps using the Screen Time framework. InstaBlock is a local network filter that targets video servers inside Instagram and TikTok.

Can I use InstaBlock together with Screen Time?

Yes. They work at different levels and don't conflict.
